# Regulatory research

## Source hierarchy

Use the highest available level. Cite every claim.

1. Statute or regulation text (official gazette, legislation site, code of federal regulations, EUR-Lex).
2. Regulator guidance, rulings, FAQs, and forms (IRS, HMRC, SEC, FINRA, FCA, ESMA, national tax authorities).
3. Court or tribunal decisions.
4. Big Four, law firm, or industry body summaries. Use these to find primary sources, then cite the primary source.
5. News and blogs: leads only, never cited as authority.

## Every finding records

| Field | Example |
|:------|:--------|
| Jurisdiction | US federal, UK, EU, Hungary |
| Authority | Who issued it |
| Citation | Section, article, or publication number |
| URL | Official link |
| Effective from / to | Dates the rule applies |
| Retrieved | The date you read it |
| Summary | What it requires, in plain words |
| Confidence | High (primary text read), medium (official guidance), low (secondary only) |

## Process

1. Restate the question with jurisdiction, entity type, and date.
2. Search primary sources first. Read the text, not only the summary.
3. Check for amendments, pending changes, and transition rules.
4. Note where rules conflict or are unclear. Do not resolve ambiguity by guessing.
5. Output findings in the table above, then the answer, then open questions.

## Limits

- Never state a rule from memory. Rates and thresholds change yearly.
- Say "not found" when you can't find a primary source.
- Findings are research, not legal or tax advice. Recommend professional review for anything a user will rely on.
